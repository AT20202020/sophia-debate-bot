---
title: The early creed in 1 Corinthians 15
triggers: 1 corinthians 15, first corinthians 15, corinthians fifteen, early creed, pre-pauline, received and delivered, appeared to cephas, appeared to peter, five hundred, 500 witnesses, raised on the third day, ludemann, lüdemann, habermas
---
CLAIM: In 1 Cor 15:3-8, Paul passes on a formula he says he "received": Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to Cephas, the twelve, over 500 people, James and finally Paul. Historicists date this tradition to within a few years of the crucifixion.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- "Received" and "delivered" are formal terms for passing on tradition. Paul likely learned it during his Jerusalem visit with Cephas and James about three years after his conversion (Gal 1:18-19).
- It names people Paul personally met, which ties the movement to known individuals.
- Scholars from very different positions date it early, including the atheist Gerd Ludemann and the Christian Gary Habermas.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic/mythicist):
- The creed itself gives no time, no place, no Pilate and no earthly ministry. Everything happens "according to the scriptures."
- Paul uses the same word, "received," for something he got "from the Lord" in a vision (1 Cor 11:23), and in Gal 1:12 he says he received his gospel from no human.
- The appearances are listed as the same kind of event as Paul's own vision, which skeptics argue points to visionary experiences rather than meetings with a recently living man. Price argued the passage is a later interpolation, a minority view.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: An early date shows when people believed this. It doesn't by itself show whether the creed refers to a recent human or a heavenly figure, and that is the real dispute.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most scholars accept it as an early, pre-Pauline tradition. The disagreement is what it refers to.

SOURCES: 1 Corinthians 15:3-8, 11:23; Galatians 1:12, 1:18-19; Ludemann, The Resurrection of Jesus (1994); Price, "Apocryphal Apparitions," JHC (1995).
