CLAIM: Christianity began with belief in a heavenly being named Jesus, crucified by demonic powers in a spiritual realm and known through visions and scripture. The Gospels later placed him on earth as allegory. This is the main mythicist theory.

CASE FOR (mythicist):
- Carrier (On the Historicity of Jesus, 2014, peer-reviewed) runs a Bayesian analysis and puts the odds of a historical Jesus at about 1 in 3 at his most generous estimate.
- The Ascension of Isaiah describes a divine figure descending through the heavens and being killed by the "god of this world."
- Philo describes a heavenly "firstborn" called "Rising," drawn from Zechariah 6:12, where the high priest is named Jesus. Carrier reads this as a pre-Christian Jewish heavenly Jesus figure.
- Jesus scores very high on the Rank-Raglan scale of mythic heroes, which Carrier uses to set a low starting probability.
- Lataster (Questioning the Historicity of Jesus, 2019) argues for agnosticism rather than a firm no.

CASE AGAINST (historicist):
- No ancient source states the celestial-only belief outright; it's reconstructed by reading Paul a certain way.
- The relevant parts of the Ascension of Isaiah are hard to date and some versions include an earthly birth. The Philo passage names no Jesus and no crucifixion.
- Critics including McGrath, Casey and Gullotta (2017) argue the Rank-Raglan reference class is arbitrary and the Bayesian inputs are subjective.
- Ancient opponents of Christianity, such as Celsus around 175, attacked Jesus' character but never argued he didn't exist.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Mythicists answer that by Celsus's time the Gospels were circulating, and point to Trypho in Justin's Dialogue (around 155) saying Christians "invented a Christ." Historicists read that as a messiah claim, not a denial of existence.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: A small minority position. Peer-reviewed defenses (Carrier, Lataster) and peer-reviewed critiques (Gullotta) both exist.

SOURCES: Carrier (2014); Lataster (2019); Doherty, The Jesus Puzzle (1999); Ascension of Isaiah 9-11; Philo, On the Confusion of Tongues 62-63; Justin, Dialogue with Trypho 8; Gullotta, JSHJ (2017).
