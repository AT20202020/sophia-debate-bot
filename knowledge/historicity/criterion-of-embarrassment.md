CLAIM: Early Christians reported things that embarrassed them, especially a crucified messiah and Jesus being baptized by John. Historicists argue nobody invents details that hurt their own cause, so these likely happened.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Jews expected a victorious messiah; Paul admits a crucified one was "a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles" (1 Cor 1:23).
- The Gospels show growing unease with the baptism: in Matthew, John objects (Matt 3:14); John's Gospel never describes the baptism at all. Ehrman argues that pattern only makes sense if the event was already known.
- Ehrman (Did Jesus Exist?, 2012) treats the crucified messiah as among the strongest arguments for a real Jesus.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic/mythicist):
- New Testament scholars themselves have challenged these criteria. Le Donne and Keith's edited volume (2012) argues the authenticity criteria can't do the work they were built for.
- What embarrassed outsiders may have been the point for insiders: Paul preaches the crucifixion as central, not as something to hide. Isaiah 53's suffering servant gave a scriptural model for a suffering messiah.
- Carrier argues a crucified messiah could come from scripture and visions without any real execution.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists answer that Isaiah 53 was not read as being about a messiah before Christians; skeptics dispute that. Both sides should note the system prompt's rule: when using this criterion, say in the same breath that its own critics dispute it.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Widely used in historical-Jesus work, but its reliability is openly debated inside the field, not just by mythicists.

SOURCES: 1 Corinthians 1:23; Mark 1:9; Matthew 3:13-15; Isaiah 52:13-53:12; Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012); Le Donne and Keith (eds.), Jesus, Criteria, and the Demise of Authenticity (2012).
