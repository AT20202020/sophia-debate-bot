---
title: James, "the brother of the Lord" (Galatians 1:19)
triggers: brother of the lord, lord's brother, james the brother, brother of jesus, galatians 1, galatians one, gal 1:19, brothers of the lord, james the just
---
CLAIM: Paul says he met "James the brother of the Lord" in Jerusalem (Gal 1:19), about three years after his conversion. If James was Jesus' biological brother, Paul personally knew a member of Jesus' family.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Paul is writing in the 50s CE, the earliest surviving Christian source, about a man he met face to face.
- Mark 6:3 independently names a James among Jesus' brothers, and Josephus (Antiquities 20.200) mentions "James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ."
- 1 Cor 9:5 refers to "the brothers of the Lord" as a known group alongside the apostles and Cephas.
- Ehrman (Did Jesus Exist?, 2012) calls this among the strongest pieces of evidence: it's hard to have a brother if you never existed.

CASE AGAINST (mythicist):
- Paul calls all believers "brothers" (adelphoi), and Rom 8:29 calls Jesus "the firstborn among many brothers." Carrier (On the Historicity of Jesus, 2014) argues "brother of the Lord" marks a baptized Christian, contrasting James with "the other apostles."
- The phrase appears only here and in 1 Cor 9:5; Paul never explains it or mentions a family relationship.
- Carrier argues the Josephus phrase "who was called Christ" is a later scribal addition and that the James there was the brother of Jesus son of Damneus, a high priest named in the same passage.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists answer that Paul never uses "brother of the Lord" for ordinary believers anywhere else, so it isn't his normal term for a Christian. Gullotta (2017) critiques Carrier's reading directly.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most New Testament scholars, including non-Christians such as Ehrman, read it as a biological brother. Carrier, Doherty and Price argue for the cultic reading, a minority view.

SOURCES: Galatians 1:18-19; 1 Corinthians 9:5; Mark 6:3; Josephus, Antiquities 20.200; Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012); Carrier, On the Historicity of Jesus (2014); Gullotta, "On Richard Carrier's Doubts," JSHJ (2017).
