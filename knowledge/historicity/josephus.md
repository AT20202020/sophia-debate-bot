---
title: Josephus (Testimonium Flavianum and the James passage)
triggers: josephus, testimonium, flavianum, antiquities, antiquities of the jews, 18.63, 20.200, agapius, whealey, olson, eusebius
---
CLAIM: Josephus, a Jewish historian writing around 93-94 CE (Antiquities 18.63-64), mentions Jesus as a wise man crucified by Pilate. Historicists treat it as non-Christian evidence; skeptics argue it is wholly or largely a Christian addition.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Most scholars accept a partial-authenticity view: Christians embellished it ("he was the Christ," the resurrection line) but a neutral core is Josephus. Meier (A Marginal Jew, 1991) reconstructs that core.
- A 10th-century Arabic version (Agapius) reads more neutrally, which Whealey (Josephus on Jesus, 2003) argues reflects an earlier, less edited text.
- Antiquities 20.200 separately calls James "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ," a phrase Christians would not naturally write. Most scholars accept it.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic/mythicist):
- The passage is not quoted by anyone until Eusebius around 324 CE. Origen (c. 248) read Josephus and says he did not believe Jesus was the Christ, which suggests his copy lacked the current text.
- Olson (CBQ, 1999) argues the language is Eusebius's own. Carrier argues the whole passage is an interpolation.
- Carrier argues "who was called Christ" in 20.200 is a later marginal note and that the James there was the brother of Jesus son of Damneus, named a few lines later.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Even full authenticity only shows what Josephus heard 60 years later; he was born after the crucifixion. Both sides agree he is not a contemporary witness.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Partial authenticity of 18.63 is the majority view; full interpolation is a significant minority view; full authenticity has almost no support. Authenticity of 20.200 is the majority view; Carrier and some others dispute it.

SOURCES: Josephus, Antiquities 18.63-64, 20.200; Origen, Against Celsus 1.47; Meier, A Marginal Jew vol. 1 (1991); Whealey (2003); Olson, CBQ 61 (1999); Carrier, On the Historicity of Jesus (2014).
