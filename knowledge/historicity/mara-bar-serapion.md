---
title: Mara bar Serapion's letter ("the wise king")
triggers: mara bar serapion, mara bar sarapion, mara, serapion, sarapion, wise king, syriac letter, samosata
---
CLAIM: A Syriac letter from Mara bar Serapion to his son mentions three wise men killed unjustly: Socrates by the Athenians, Pythagoras by the Samians, and a "wise king" by the Jews, whose kingdom was then taken away. Historicists read the wise king as Jesus, attested by a non-Christian.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Most scholars take the author to be a non-Christian, often described as a Stoic-leaning philosopher, so the reference comes from outside the movement.
- Many date it shortly after 73 CE, when Rome annexed his home region (Commagene), which would make it among the earliest non-Christian references.
- The "kingdom taken away" fits the destruction of Jerusalem in 70, and "the new laws he laid down" fits a figure whose teaching lived on.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- The letter never names Jesus. "Wise king" is an inference.
- Its date is genuinely disputed: proposals run from the 70s to the 3rd century. It survives only in a single much later manuscript.
- It blames "the Jews," not Rome, for the death. Skeptics argue that matches Christian telling of the story, so Mara may simply be repeating what Christians said, which shows belief, not independent knowledge.

ACCURACY NOTES: The view that the letter is a Christian forgery is not a standard scholarly position; don't state it as fact. Its philosophical school is uncertain; don't assert one.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most scholars think the wise king is probably Jesus and the author probably non-Christian. Few treat it as strong evidence, because of the uncertain date and the lack of a name.

SOURCES: Mara bar Serapion, Letter (British Library Add. MS 14658); Merz and Tieleman (eds.), Mara bar Sarapion in Context (Brill, 2012); Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the New Testament (2000).
