---
title: The apostles died for their testimony
triggers: martyr, martyrs, martyrdom, died for, die for a lie, willing to die, apostles died, fate of the apostles, executed for their faith, sincerity, sincere
---
CLAIM: The apostles were persecuted and killed for saying they knew Jesus and saw him risen. People don't die for something they know is a lie, so their testimony is sincere.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Paul describes his own beatings and imprisonments, and he persecuted the movement himself before joining it (Gal 1:13; 2 Cor 11:23-27).
- Some deaths are reasonably attested: James son of Zebedee (Acts 12:2), James the brother of Jesus (Josephus, Antiquities 20.200), and Peter and Paul (1 Clement 5, around 96 CE).
- If Peter and James knew Jesus personally and still risked death, historicists argue it is hard to see them following someone who never lived.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- For most of the twelve, the death stories are later legends. Even the Christian scholar Sean McDowell (The Fate of the Apostles, 2015) rates the evidence as strong only for a few.
- Dying for a belief shows sincerity, not truth. People of many religions have died for incompatible beliefs; the 39 members of the Heaven's Gate group died in 1997 for a belief that was plainly false.
- On the existence question specifically, mythicists say the first leaders sincerely believed in a Christ they knew through visions (1 Cor 15:5-8). Sincerity is compatible with that.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: This argument is usually about the resurrection, not existence. If the debate drifts into the resurrection, say so and bring it back to whether a man existed.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Scholars agree the earliest leaders were sincere and some were killed. Most don't treat martyrdom as evidence for historicity on its own.

SOURCES: Galatians 1:13; 2 Corinthians 11:23-27; Acts 12:2; 1 Clement 5; Josephus, Antiquities 20.200; McDowell, The Fate of the Apostles (2015).
