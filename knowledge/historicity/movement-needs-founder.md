title: A movement this early needs a real founder

CLAIM: Christianity appears within a few years of the supposed crucifixion, led by named people in Jerusalem. Historicists argue a real founder is the simplest explanation, as with most religious movements.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Paul names living leaders, Peter (Cephas) and James, whom he met in Jerusalem within a few years of joining (Gal 1:18-19, 2:9).
- Most religious movements with a recent founder trace to a real person: Muhammad, Joseph Smith, the Baha'i founders.
- A recently executed messiah is a strange thing to invent from scratch, which overlaps with the criterion of embarrassment.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- Movements can start with real people who report visions. On the mythicist reading, Peter, James and Paul were the founders, and their revelations of a heavenly Christ were the start (1 Cor 15:5-8, Gal 1:12).
- Carrier cites the John Frum cargo cult in Vanuatu (1930s onward), centered on a figure who may never have existed, as a modern example of a movement built around an invented founder.
- Many traditions trace themselves to founders historians doubt, such as Romulus or Moses.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists answer that John Frum is debated and the parallel is loose. Skeptics answer that the existence of real leaders isn't in question; the question is whether there was a Jesus behind them.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most scholars find a real founder the more likely explanation. Mythicists argue the visionary leaders explain the evidence equally well.

SOURCES: Galatians 1:12, 1:18-19, 2:9; 1 Corinthians 15:5-8; Carrier, On the Historicity of Jesus (2014); Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012).
