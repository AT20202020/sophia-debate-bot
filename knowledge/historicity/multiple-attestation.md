CLAIM: Jesus is attested in several independent sources: Paul, Mark, Q (material shared by Matthew and Luke but not in Mark), M and L (material only in Matthew or Luke), John, plus Josephus and Tacitus. Independent sources agreeing is strong evidence.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Ehrman (Did Jesus Exist?, 2012) counts many independent streams of tradition within a few decades of the death, more than for most ancient figures.
- Paul's letters are clearly independent of the Gospels, which were written later.
- Multiple attestation is a standard tool in history generally, not something invented for Jesus.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- Q, M and L are hypothetical sources. No copy of any of them exists. Lataster argues historicists lean on documents that may never have existed.
- Goodacre (The Case Against Q, 2002) argues Luke copied Matthew, which would remove Q entirely. Matthew and Luke both copy Mark, so they aren't independent of it.
- Whether John knew the other Gospels is debated. All the Christian sources come from one movement and may share oral tradition, so "independent" is doing a lot of work.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists answer that even without Q, Paul and Mark are independent of each other. Skeptics answer that Paul gives almost no biographical detail (see the Paul's-silence card), so there's little to cross-check.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most scholars accept Q and multiple attestation; a significant minority reject Q. The criterion itself is debated alongside the other authenticity criteria.

SOURCES: Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012); Goodacre, The Case Against Q (2002); Lataster, Questioning the Historicity of Jesus (2019).
