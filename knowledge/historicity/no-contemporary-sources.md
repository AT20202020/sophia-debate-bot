---
title: No contemporary sources (the argument from silence)
triggers: contemporary source, contemporary sources, contemporary record, contemporary records, eyewitness, eyewitnesses, philo, justus of tiberias, seneca, pliny the elder, argument from silence, during his lifetime, roman records
---
CLAIM: Nothing written during Jesus' supposed lifetime mentions him. Skeptics argue writers who should have noticed him are silent.

CASE FOR (skeptic/mythicist):
- No inscription, letter, record or text from the 30s CE mentions Jesus. The earliest references are Paul's letters, from the 50s.
- Philo of Alexandria (died around 50 CE) wrote extensively about Jewish sects, about Pilate, and about a divine "Logos," yet says nothing about Jesus or Christians.
- Justus of Tiberias wrote a history of the Galilee region; the 9th-century scholar Photius, who read it, noted it never mentioned Christ.
- If the Gospels' public miracles, crowds and darkness at noon had happened, skeptics argue someone outside the movement would have recorded them.

CASE AGAINST (historicist):
- Almost no Galilean peasant preacher of the period left any contemporary trace. John the Baptist, a bigger public figure at the time, is known only from Josephus and the Gospels.
- Very little written evidence survives from 1st-century Judea at all. Silence is only telling where a writer would be expected to mention someone, and historicists argue a minor executed preacher fails that test.
- Paul's letters come within about 20-25 years of the death, which is early by ancient standards.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Mythicists reply that the silence is about more than a minor preacher: it's about the Gospel Jesus, whose miracles and fame should have been noticed. Historicists reply that argues against the miracles, not against a minimal historical Jesus.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Everyone agrees there are no contemporary sources. The disagreement is over what that shows. Most scholars treat it as unremarkable; mythicists treat Philo's silence in particular as significant.

SOURCES: Philo, Embassy to Gaius 299-305 (on Pilate); Photius, Bibliotheca 33 (on Justus); Josephus, Antiquities 18.116-119 (on John the Baptist); Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012).
