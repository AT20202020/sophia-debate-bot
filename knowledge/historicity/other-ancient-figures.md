CLAIM: Jesus is as well attested as, or better than, famous ancient figures nobody doubts. Rejecting him means applying a standard you'd never apply to anyone else.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Most people in antiquity left no trace at all. By the standard applied to ordinary people of his class, a Galilean preacher with letters about him within about 20 years is well attested.
- Comparable figures, like John the Baptist or the prophet Theudas, are known from just one or two later sources and are rarely doubted. Ehrman argues the fair comparison is with them, not with emperors.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- The famous comparisons usually fail. Alexander has coins and inscriptions from his lifetime, and his later historians (like Arrian) used accounts by his own officers. Julius Caesar wrote his own books and appears in Cicero's letters. Tiberius has coins, inscriptions and a contemporary historian. Socrates was written about by people who knew him (Plato, Xenophon) and mocked on stage during his lifetime (Aristophanes, Clouds).
- Counting New Testament manuscripts measures how well a text was copied, not whether its subject existed.
- An unusual claim deserves more scrutiny: nobody's case for Alexander depends on miracle stories.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists concede the emperor comparison is bad apologetics and fall back to the "ordinary preacher" comparison. Skeptics reply that John the Baptist is also known mainly through Christian texts plus one passage in Josephus.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Historians generally reject the "better than Caesar" claim as an overstatement. The narrower point, that Jesus is reasonably attested for someone of his type, is the mainstream view.

SOURCES: Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012); Arrian, Anabasis; Aristophanes, Clouds (423 BCE); Josephus, Antiquities 18.116-119 (John the Baptist), 20.97-98 (Theudas).
