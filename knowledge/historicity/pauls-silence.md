---
title: Paul's silence about an earthly Jesus
triggers: paul's silence, paul never mentions, pauline epistles, paul's letters, letters of paul, apostle paul, born of a woman, seed of david, rulers of this age, revelation from jesus, galatians 1:11
---
CLAIM: Paul's letters, the earliest Christian writings, say almost nothing about Jesus' life on earth. Mythicists argue this fits best with an early belief in a heavenly Christ, known through visions and scripture.

CASE FOR (mythicist):
- Paul never mentions a miracle, a parable, Nazareth, Mary by name, John the Baptist, a trial, or Pilate.
- He says his gospel came "through a revelation of Jesus Christ," not from any human (Gal 1:11-12), and lists the resurrection appearances, including his own vision, as the same kind of event (1 Cor 15:3-8).
- Events happen "according to the scriptures" (1 Cor 15:3-4), suggesting scripture, not memory, as the source.
- 1 Cor 2:8 says "the rulers of this age" crucified the Lord of glory, which Doherty and Carrier read as demonic powers rather than Roman officials.

CASE AGAINST (historicist):
- Paul does give earthly details: "born of a woman, born under the law" (Gal 4:4), "descended from David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3), a meal "on the night he was handed over" (1 Cor 11:23), and brothers (Gal 1:19; 1 Cor 9:5).
- He cites a "command of the Lord" on divorce (1 Cor 7:10), separating it from his own opinion.
- His letters answer specific problems in specific churches; they aren't biographies, and he assumes his readers already know the story. An argument from silence is weak evidence.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Carrier argues Paul uses "made" (genomenos), not "born," in Gal 4:4 and Rom 1:3, which fits a cosmic or allegorical reading. Historicists answer that genomenos is ordinary Greek for being born.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Most scholars read Paul as referring to a recently crucified human. Doherty, Carrier and Lataster argue the silence supports a celestial origin. Britt and Wingo (Christ Before Jesus) dispute when some of the letters were written at all, which is a separate question.

SOURCES: Galatians 1:11-12, 4:4; Romans 1:3; 1 Corinthians 2:8, 7:10, 11:23, 15:3-8; Doherty, The Jesus Puzzle (1999); Carrier, On the Historicity of Jesus (2014); Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist? (2012).
