title: Pliny the Younger and Suetonius

CLAIM: Two more Roman writers mention Christians: Pliny the Younger (around 112 CE) and Suetonius (around 121 CE). Apologists often list them alongside Tacitus as non-Christian evidence for Jesus.

CASE FOR (historicist):
- Pliny, governor of Bithynia, reports to the emperor Trajan that Christians "sing a hymn to Christ as to a god" (Letters 10.96). It shows an established movement centered on Christ within about 80 years.
- Suetonius says Claudius expelled the Jews from Rome because they kept rioting "at the instigation of Chrestus" (Claudius 25.4). Acts 18:2 also mentions this expulsion, around 49 CE. Many read "Chrestus" as a garbled "Christus," pointing to disputes over Christ in Rome's Jewish community within 20 years of the crucifixion.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- Pliny says nothing about Jesus' life, only what Christians in his province believed and did. That is evidence of the movement, which nobody disputes.
- "Chrestus" was a common name, especially for slaves, and Suetonius describes him as present in Rome stirring things up. It may refer to a local agitator, not to Jesus at all.
- Suetonius' separate mention of Christians punished under Nero (Nero 16.2) also says nothing about Jesus.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Historicists generally agree these two add little on their own and lean on Tacitus and Josephus instead. Mythicists agree they show belief, not history. The real dispute is mostly about Tacitus.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Broad agreement that Pliny is evidence for early Christian practice, not for Jesus' life. Whether "Chrestus" means Christ is split.

SOURCES: Pliny the Younger, Letters 10.96-97; Suetonius, Claudius 25.4 and Nero 16.2; Acts 18:2; Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the New Testament (2000).
