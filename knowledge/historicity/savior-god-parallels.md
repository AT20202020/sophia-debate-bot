CLAIM: Many gods before Jesus were said to die and rise or be born of virgins, so mythicists argue the Jesus story was built from a common religious pattern rather than history.

CASE FOR (mythicist):
- Tryggve Mettinger (The Riddle of Resurrection, 2001), a mainstream scholar, concluded that dying-and-rising deities did exist in the ancient Near East before Christianity.
- Carrier points to Inanna (a goddess killed, hung on a hook, and restored after three days), Osiris, and Romulus (who vanished, was proclaimed a god, and appeared to a witness afterward, as Plutarch tells it).
- The early Christian writer Justin Martyr (First Apology 21-22, around 155) himself acknowledged similarities to pagan stories and blamed demons for imitating Christ in advance.

CASE AGAINST (historicist):
- Most popular parallels are false. No ancient source has Horus born of a virgin on December 25 with twelve disciples, Krishna crucified, or Mithras born of a virgin (he was born from a rock). These come from 19th-century writers like Kersey Graves and films like Zeitgeist, not from scholarship.
- Jonathan Z. Smith argued "dying and rising gods" is a modern category forced onto very different myths. Osiris, for example, rules the underworld rather than returning to life on earth.
- Parallels with mythic figures, even real ones, don't show a figure wasn't historical; legends grow around real people too.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Mythicists answer that the defensible parallels (Inanna, Romulus, Osiris) are enough to show the motif was available. Historicists answer that the earliest Christians were Jews drawing on Jewish scripture, not pagan myth.

ACCURACY NOTES: Never use the Horus, Krishna or Mithras "virgin birth / December 25 / twelve disciples / crucified" claims; they are wrong and a prepared opponent will catch them. Stick to Inanna, Osiris and Romulus, attributed.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Contested. Smith's skepticism about the category was dominant for decades; Mettinger revived the case. Very few scholars accept the popular copycat lists.

SOURCES: Mettinger, The Riddle of Resurrection (2001); Smith, "Dying and Rising Gods," Encyclopedia of Religion (1987); Justin Martyr, First Apology 21-22; Plutarch, Life of Romulus 27-28; Carrier, On the Historicity of Jesus (2014).
