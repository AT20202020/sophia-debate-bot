CLAIM: For G.W.F. Hegel, logic is not a neutral formal tool for testing arguments. In the Science of Logic (1812-16) it is metaphysics: thought and being form an active unity, and concepts develop through contradiction (dialectic) toward the "Absolute Idea."

HOW THEISTS USE HEGEL:
- If reality is at bottom rational and mind-like, as Hegel holds, theists argue this fits a God better than blind matter. Hegel himself said religion and philosophy share the same content, religion in picture form (Lectures on the Philosophy of Religion).
- Against "logic is just a tool": if logic is the structure of being, a purely formal refutation of God arguments misses the point.

HOW SKEPTICS USE HEGEL:
- Hegel's Absolute is not clearly the personal God of Christianity. Many read him as a pantheist or panentheist, and his "God" as reason working through history.
- The Left Hegelians turned him against religion: Ludwig Feuerbach (The Essence of Christianity, 1841) argued God is humanity's projection of its own nature, and Marx followed.

ON MORALITY: In the Philosophy of Right (1820), Hegel criticizes Kant's moral law as an "empty formalism" and locates ethics in "ethical life" (Sittlichkeit): family, civil society and the state. That grounds morality in human institutions and history, not divine commands.

ACCURACY NOTES: "Thesis-antithesis-synthesis" is a popular simplification Hegel didn't use as his formula; don't attribute it to him. Don't claim flatly that he rejected the law of non-contradiction - how his dialectic relates to formal logic is debated.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Hegel is read as everything from a Christian philosopher to a secular theorist of reason in history. Analytic critics (Russell; Popper, The Open Society and Its Enemies, 1945) called him obscure; he has had a revival since the 1970s.

SOURCES: Hegel, Science of Logic (1812-16), Phenomenology of Spirit (1807), Philosophy of Right (1820); Feuerbach, The Essence of Christianity (1841).
