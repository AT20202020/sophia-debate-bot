CLAIM: (1) If God does not exist, objective moral values and duties do not exist. (2) Objective moral values and duties do exist. (3) So God exists. This is William Lane Craig's popular form; C.S. Lewis (Mere Christianity, 1952) made an earlier version.

CASE FOR (theist):
- Most people, including most atheists, are confident some things are really wrong, not just disliked. Craig treats that moral experience as evidence for premise 2.
- Craig argues that without God, morality reduces to evolution and social convention, which can't produce binding duties: on atheism we are, in his phrase, "accidental by-products of nature."
- A moral law, Lewis argues, implies a moral lawgiver.

CASE AGAINST (skeptic):
- Premise 1 is the weak point. Many philosophers defend objective morality without God (see the "objective morality without God" card). The theist has to show those views fail, not just assert premise 1.
- The Euthyphro dilemma asks whether God grounds goodness or merely reports it (see that card).
- Anti-realists deny premise 2, so the argument has nothing to work on for them.
- Even if it worked, it gives a source of moral facts, not the Christian God in particular: no Trinity, no incarnation.
- Believers disagree deeply on moral questions (slavery, divorce, war), which skeptics argue undercuts the idea that God's will gives clear moral knowledge.

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Craig separates moral ONTOLOGY (what grounds morality) from moral EPISTEMOLOGY (how we know it). He agrees atheists can know and do what's right; his claim is that only God grounds it. Don't answer "atheists can be good people" - he grants that.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: In the 2020 PhilPapers survey of philosophers, most accepted or leaned toward moral realism and most were atheists, so many philosophers reject premise 1. Theists such as Craig and Robert Adams defend it.

SOURCES: Craig, Reasonable Faith (3rd ed. 2008); Lewis, Mere Christianity (1952); Bourget and Chalmers, "Philosophers on Philosophy: The 2020 PhilPapers Survey" (2023).
