CLAIM: The theist says that without God there's no foundation for objective morality. Secular moral realists reply that moral facts can be objective without God.

CASE FOR SECULAR REALISM:
- Erik Wielenberg (Robust Ethics, 2014) argues some moral truths are basic and necessary, like mathematical or logical truths, and need no further ground. "Gratuitous cruelty is wrong" doesn't become more true by adding God.
- Russ Shafer-Landau (Moral Realism: A Defence, 2003) and Derek Parfit (On What Matters, 2011) defend non-religious moral realism.
- Naturalist realists tie moral facts to facts about well-being. Sam Harris (The Moral Landscape, 2010) offers a popular version, though professional philosophers often criticize it.

CASE AGAINST (theist):
- Craig calls Wielenberg's view "atheistic moral Platonism" and asks why abstract moral facts would bind anyone or why evolved creatures would track them.
- Evolutionary debunking: Sharon Street ("A Darwinian Dilemma for Realist Theories of Value," 2006) argues evolution shaped our moral beliefs for survival, not truth, so secular realists can't explain why our beliefs would match independent moral facts. Theists say God solves this; Street's argument is aimed at realism generally.
- Harris is often criticized for assuming well-being matters rather than showing it (the is-ought gap).

REPLIES TO WATCH FOR: Wielenberg replies that theists face the same "why does it bind us" question, just one step back. Remember Sophia's role: she doesn't need to defend any of these views. Their existence shows the theist's premise "no God, no objective morality" is contested, and the theist has to defeat them.

WHERE SCHOLARS STAND: Secular moral realism is a mainstream position in academic philosophy, not a fringe one. Whether it can answer the grounding and debunking objections is actively debated.

SOURCES: Wielenberg, Robust Ethics (2014); Shafer-Landau, Moral Realism: A Defence (2003); Parfit, On What Matters (2011); Harris, The Moral Landscape (2010); Street, Philosophical Studies 127 (2006).
